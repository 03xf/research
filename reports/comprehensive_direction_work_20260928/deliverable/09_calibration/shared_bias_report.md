# Shared systematic-bias diagnostic

The key counterexample is B3_N: nominal three-ray median residual 1.185 m, LRF horizontal error 13.283 m; background-fitted visual variants still miss by 11.535–12.676 m. In C1, three LOUO results are stable within 0.5 m of their median and still carry roughly 11 m absolute error. Low residual and low subset spread therefore fail as absolute-accuracy certificates.

I ran one-factor-at-a-time, shared-across-camera sweeps over uniform yaw/pitch offsets, focal scale, and GPS east/north offsets on B1_F1/B3_N/B3_S. Best mean three-site errors were: uniform_yaw_offset_deg=3 (mean 5.93 m; B3_N 11.86 m), uniform_pitch_offset_deg=5 (mean 5.17 m; B3_N 11.66 m), uniform_focal_scale=1.3 (mean 4.15 m; B3_N 10.13 m), uniform_gps_east_offset_m=-2 (mean 5.78 m; B3_N 14.21 m), uniform_gps_north_offset_m=0 (mean 6.00 m; B3_N 13.28 m). These are post-hoc checks on three linked sites, not calibration estimates. A shared parameter that improves pooled fit can trade errors among sites. The data do not identify whether B3_N residual bias is due to camera model, boresight, focal convention, platform/gimbal frame, GPS/altitude, time offset, manual source point, or LRF target semantics. Mode/drone/event are confounded, and no independent optical-axis target exists. Per-camera and mode-specific offsets are therefore not identifiable from these observations. The sweep does not establish a causal explanation; it only tests whether a simple global offset explains all sites consistently.
